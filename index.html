HISTORICAL FOUNDATIONS

The Cultural Home of the Doligbe

The Dorimon/Dolimon people, also known as the Doligbe of Ghana, trace their royal origins to Yendi, the ancient seat of the Dagbon kingdom in northern Ghana. Their history is an epic road — from Yendi westward through Doli, across the Black Volta river, into Gbono/Bouna in present-day Côte d'Ivoire, and back into Ghana through the settlements of Dontanga and Donkoru, Bieli, and finally Dorimon.

Part of the wider Mole-Dagbani world, Dorimon chieftaincy carries the Dagbon inheritance: the skin as the sacred symbol of authority, gate succession, palace order, and a delicate balance between royal chiefs and land custodians called Tendamba. Today, Wechiau and Dorimon stand as the two paramouncy seats in the Wa West District, preserving centuries of living customary covenants.

"The skin belongs to the whole tradition: gates, Tendamba, palace, elders, and people together." — Bieli Customary enskinment Covenants

Late 17th Century

The Departure from Dagbon (Yendi)

Naa Zokuli, a ruler of Dagbon, led a westward expedition in search of wealth and territory, producing the Hanga, Wala/Dorimon, and Koulango branches. They settled first at Doli (near Bole), which birthed the name Dolimon, before being forced across the Volta by the Gonja warrior Jakpa.

Dagbon Royal Origin Coordinates: Yendi Kingdom
Circa 1690

The Centralized Kingdom of Bouna

In Gbano/Gbono (Bouna, Côte d'Ivoire), Naa Zokuli formed a union with Mantou, sister of King Haïngère of the Lorhon. Their son Bounkani founded the Kingdom of Bouna, described as the first centralized state in Côte d'Ivoire. Following Zokuli's disappearance in Bouna Lake, a gold-adorned crocodile appeared, and a sacred Kuli vabu funeral was held in Dagbon.

Lorhon Union Settlement: Gbono / Bouna
Circa 1720

Return to Ghana & Bieli Covenants

The contingent returned eastward, settling among deep-rooted land custodians (Tendamba) in Bieli. The founding brothers, Dakpa and Gango, established the Dakpayiri and Gangoyiri gates. The new settlement around the palace grew as Dolimon, carrying the memory of Doli and fortified by the Bieli Tendamba.

Double Royal Gates Location: Bieli & Dorimon
1983 & 2025

Paramountcy & Validated Succession

Dorimon achieved formal paramountcy status on 15 January 1983 under Paramount Chief Naa Abudu Mumuni. On 15 May 2025, the Upper West Regional House of Chiefs validated the enskinment of Naa Seidu Tungbani Salinbile II as the Paramount Chief, securing dynastic continuity.

ARCHITECTURAL PRESERVATION

The Proposed Conservation Project

To secure the physical home of this timeless archive, the Dorimon Traditional Council has partnered with global architectural preservationists to design the new Paala Palace Conservation Center.

The proposed design meticulously preserves the historic Sudanese-style adobe mud-brick structures while seamlessly introducing an underground climate-controlled archival vault and research library. The materials will be sourced locally—using indigenous stabilized earth, mahogany timber framing, and thermal solar cooling—to ensure the site remains a sustainable, living monument to West African architectural heritage.

CHRONOLOGICAL SUCCESSION

The Royal Dynasty Succession

Witness the verified chain of royal succession governing the Dorimon traditional throne. Toggle between the royal gates to trace the lineage and view the elders' comments.

The Royal Gates

01
Circa 1795 – 1815

Naa Toriwonye

Gate: Gangoyiri Royal Gate

One of the early enskinned sovereigns after the founding brothers Dakpa and Gango. Formed extensive trade and military covenants along the Black Volta river corridor, stabilizing the early Dolimon settlements.

02
Circa 1815 – 1835

Naa Badabay

Gate: Dakpayiri Royal Gate

Consolidated the gate's land domain (gaara) around Dontanga and Kogle. Standardized the rotation covenants of the skin and reinforced enskinment fortification sequences at Bieli.

03
Circa 1835 – 1860

Naa Zinmara Serber

Gate: Gangoyiri Royal Gate

Sovereign of great expansion. Sent his son Daari Zinmara eastward to discover fertile lands at Nahari, giving his sons the ancestral shrine Adomakuma and establishing the female Tugbani ritual sequence.

04
Circa 1890 – 1915

Naa Sohiwminye Danaa Gore II

Gate: Dakpayiri Royal Gate

Reigned during late 19th-century regional transitions. Successfully navigated skin integrity and custom preservation against external shifts while protecting the Bieli Tendamba boundaries.

05
1958 – 1983

Naa Abudu Mumuni

Gate: Gangoyiri Royal Gate

Reigned during the late 20th century. Led the traditional council with profound wisdom and officially secured formal paramountcy status for the Dorimon Skin on 15 January 1983.

06
2025 – Present

Naa Seidu Tungbani Salinbile II

Gate: Dakpayiri Royal Gate

The validated reigning Paramount Chief of the Dorimon Traditional Area. Declared validly appointed by the Upper West Regional House of Chiefs on 15 May 2025, continuing the historical rotation.
